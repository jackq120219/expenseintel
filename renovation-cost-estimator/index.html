Repair / renovation

The quote is a scope price, not always the final project cost.

A renovation estimate becomes more useful when it separates the quoted work from the things that can move the total: exclusions, permits, change orders, material upgrades, schedule problems, discovered conditions and the operating cost of what you install.

Build the estimate in layers

  • Base scope: the contractor quote or project budget you already have.
  • Known extras: permits, design, engineering, disposal, temporary work or owner-provided items not included in the quote.
  • Contingency: a visible reserve for uncertainty rather than pretending the base quote is guaranteed.
  • Change-order exposure: specific exclusions, unknown site conditions or selections that could move the contract.
  • Recurring impact: service, energy, maintenance or operating costs created by the finished project.

Contingency is not the same as padding

A contingency should correspond to uncertainty. A well-defined replacement with verified conditions may need a different reserve than an invasive remodel with hidden conditions, incomplete selections or unresolved engineering. ExpenseIntel treats the reserve as an explicit assumption so you can change it and see the effect immediately.

What to verify before signing

  • Exactly what labor and materials are included
  • Allowances and what happens when actual selections exceed them
  • Permits, inspections and design responsibility
  • Demolition, disposal, restoration and cleanup
  • Schedule assumptions and delay responsibility
  • Change-order process and markup
  • Warranties, service obligations and exclusions
