Data / What belongs in the model
Only if it changes the expense picture.

Less property trivia. More cost intelligence.

ExpenseIntel deliberately avoids becoming another generic property-data portal. A data layer earns its place only when it helps estimate, explain, compare or forecast a meaningful expense.

01 / Evidence architecture

Every number gets an evidence trail.

The ExpenseIntel model is intentionally mixed-source. Some inputs are directly verified, some are authoritative market averages, some are statistical building benchmarks, and some remain explicit model assumptions. The interface should never blur those categories together.

01 / PLACEResolve the location.

Canonical address, coordinates, city, state, ZIP, county and tract.

02 / MARKETPull current cost signals.

State energy prices today; utility-level tariff data next.

03 / BUILDINGEstimate consumption.

Building-use intensity, size and eventually richer property attributes.

04 / DECISIONExpose uncertainty.

Range, confidence, source freshness, drivers and forward movement.

02 / Financially relevant signals

Wide data. Narrow purpose.

ExpenseIntel can ingest many sources without becoming a generic site-intelligence product. The filter stays financial: if a signal cannot move an expense, confidence interval or comparison, it does not belong.

ENERGY / LIVE

Retail electricity price

State and end-use sector price, prior-year change and eventually exact utility tariff structure.

ENERGY / LIVE

Natural gas price

State end-use price combined with building-use gas intensity rather than a generic percentage of total cost.

BUILDING / BENCHMARK

Consumption intensity

kWh and gas use per square foot by property use, with CBECS-backed commercial categories and labeled bridges where data is weaker.

PROPERTY / NEXT

Tax burden

Actual assessment and tax bill should replace broad modeled exposure wherever parcel-level records are available.

UTILITY / NEXT

Water + sewer schedule

Base fees, consumption tiers, sewer multipliers and known rate increases normalized into one annual view.

RISK / NEXT

Insurance pressure

Flood, catastrophe, construction and use signals should explain exposure without pretending to be an insurance quote.

03 / Confidence

Never make an estimate look more certain than it is.

The product becomes more trustworthy when it distinguishes directly observed information, strong estimates and lower-confidence scenario assumptions instead of hiding all three behind one polished number.

A

Observed / authoritative

Verified address, EIA published price series, future tariff schedules, tax records and user-supplied actual bills.

B

Benchmark + strong inputs

Statistical consumption intensity, normalized usage and forecasts built from high-quality upstream signals.

C

Scenario / incomplete

Costs that still depend on missing building details, fragmented local schedules or uncertain future conditions.

04 / Infrastructure

Infrastructure belongs when it has a financial consequence.

ExpenseIntel should not claim to map buried utilities or replace an engineering locate. The useful version is narrower: identify service or connection conditions only when they affect operating, upgrade or development cost.

INCLUDE

Service availability

Gas, municipal water, sewer or other service availability can materially change the economics of a site.

WhyRecurring / hookup cost
INCLUDE

Connection + upgrade risk

Potential service upgrades, capacity constraints or extension requirements belong when they create a measurable cost.

WhyCapital exposure

Precise buried-utility location

ExpenseIntel should not guess the exact depth or position of underground infrastructure and should never substitute for authorized locating.
